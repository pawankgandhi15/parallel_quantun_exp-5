ABSTRACT
Image classifcation plays a critical role across various industries, yet the rapid growth of visual data has signifcantly increased
computational complexity. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in efcient feature
extraction, but they still face trade-ofs between performance and computational cost when handling large-scale data. Recent
studies have explored integrating quantum mechanical principles into classical CNNs to enhance model expressivity. However,
existing hybrid quantum–classical convolutional architectures remain limited in terms of feature capture and computational
efciency.In thisstudy, we propose a novel hybridquantum–classical convolutional neural network (QC-CNN-Parallel), which
introduces parallel quantum and classical convolutional branches and extends the concept of multiscale convolution to hybrid
networks.Thisdesignenablesthecaptureoflargercontextualinformationduringconvolutionwhilereducingcircuitdepthand
computational overhead. Moreover, we employ parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) with expressibility, entanglement, and
discretenessmetricstoselectoptimalquantumcircuitsandfurtherenhancemodelperformance.WeevaluateQC-CNN-Parallelon
three grayscale image datasets: MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and Overhead-MNIST. Experimental results show that our model
improves average training accuracy by 4.89%, 2.77%, and 6.24% over existing hybrid quantum CNNs and classical CNNs, re-
spectively,whileexhibitingsuperiorrobustness.TheseresultsdemonstratethatQC-CNN-Parallelachievesenhancedclassifcation
performance with high computational efciency and practical potential.

3.2 | PQCs
PQCs represent a critical element of QNNs, and their archi-
tectural design can profoundly infuence the performance of
QNN models. In contrast to encoding layers, which are char-

PQCs optimize adjustable parameters to approximate target
functions, thereby mapping input features to value ranges that
correspond to diferent classes. The architecture of a PQC is
typically composed of two primary components: variational
layers and entangling layers. The variational layers generally

3.4 | Classical Dense Layer
After obtaining the feature data from the PQC output, further
processing is conducted. In the hybrid layer of the model, fea-
tures extracted by the quantum convolutional layer (PQC) are
concatenated with those from the classical convolutional layer
along the channel dimension, resulting in multichannel feature
fusion. This fusion strategy efectively harnesses the comple-
mentary strengths of both quantum and classical features,
thereby enhancing the model’s representational capacity.
The fused features are subsequently fed into a three-layer fully
connected neural network for the purpose of classifcation. A

connected layers to augment the model’s ability to capture
nonlinearrelationships.Thefnalclassifcationoutputistrained
utilizing the cross-entropy loss function, which serves to opti-
mize the model parameters.

4.3.3 | Experiment 3: Analysis of the Efects of
Noise on Model Performance
To thoroughly assess the robustness of the proposed model in
the presence of noisy conditions, we performed a series of
performancetestsacrossdiferenttypesofnoiseandcompared
theoutcomeswiththoseoftraditionalconvolutionalmodels.In
terms of data noise, to efectively isolate the efects of noise
fromthedimensionalityreductionprocess,Gaussiannoisewas

reduction. This approach more accurately simulates the in-
fuence of noise at the perceptual layer on the performance of

Inaddressingthenoiseinherenttothemodel,weexaminedthree
distinct types of noise that are applied immediately prior to the
measurement of qubits: bit-fip noise, phase-fip noise, and
depolarizing noise. The defnitions of these noise types are as
follows.
• Bit-Flip Noise: Bit-fip noise is one of the fundamental
quantumerrormodelsandcorrespondstotheapplicationof
a Pauli-X gate on a qubit with probability p. This noise
causesthe quantum state to randomly fip between |0〉and
|1〉. Its evolution can be described as
ρ⟶ (1 − p)ρ + pXρX, (25)
where X is the Pauli-X operator.
• Phase-Flip Noise: Phase-fip noise modifes the phase of
the quantum state by applying a Pauli-Z gate with proba-
bility p.Whenactingonaqubit,thephaseofthe |1〉stateis
inverted while the |0〉 state remains unchanged. Its math-
ematical expression is
ρ⟶ (1 − p)ρ + pZρZ, (26)
where Z is the Pauli-Z operator.
• Depolarizing Noise: Depolarizing noise is a widely used
generalerrormodelinquantumchannels.Withprobability
p, it replaces the quantum state with a maximally mixed
state, and with probability 1 − p, it leaves the state un-
changed. Its evolution is given by
ρ⟶ (1 − p)ρ + p3(XρX + YρY + ZρZ), (27)
where X, Y, and Z are the Pauli operators.

5 | Conclusion
Specifcally, we show that training performance is jointly
infuenced by expressibility, entangling capability, and gradient
discreteness,andthathighexpressibilityorstrongentanglement
alone does not guarantee efective optimization, particularly in
the presence of barren plateaus. Furthermore, the parallel ar-
chitecture is shown to beneft from the global nonlinear trans-
formationsinducedbythequantumbranch,whichcomplement
thelocalreceptivefeldsofclassicalconvolutionsandcollectively
enhancefeaturediscrimination.Finally,themodeldemonstrates
strong robustness under various noise conditions, which is
theoreticallyattributedtotheredundancyintroducedbyparallel
quantum–classical feature channels and the use of shallow,
hardware-efcient PQCs that mitigate noise efects on NISQ
devices. These results indicate that QC-CNN-Parallel ofers
a principled and practically viable pathway for constructing
robust and trainable hybrid quantum learning models.
Insummary,the advantagesofQC-CNN-Parallel arisenotfrom
isolated empirical observations but from the interplay of its
parallel convolutional structure, quantum kernel design, and
multimetricPQCselectionstrategy.Nevertheless,themodelstill
presents limitations that warrant future investigation.
